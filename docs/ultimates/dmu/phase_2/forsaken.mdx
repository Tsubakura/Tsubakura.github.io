> *"Intriguing. This clearly isn't your first time..."*

As you progress into phase 2, Kefka is starting to get suspicious as to how you did all mechanics flawlessly in phase 1. Almost as if your party has wiped hundreds of times to his attacks before... almost as if the fight simply restarts whenever everyone in the party dies...

> *"This is meant to be our ultimate encounter..."*

As soon as Kefka becomes targetable, the MT should immediately establish the main aggro on the boss because he will cast <Tankbuster>Ultimate Embrace</Tankbuster> soon, which is a two-man tankbuster. Unless the party has a WAR and has volunteered to invuln this one, both tanks needs to kitchen sink all their mitigations to survive this attack.

> *"Crafty little bastards, aren't you?"*

After dealing with the tankbusters, Kefka will do a few more auto attacks before he starts casting <Raidwide>Forsaken</Raidwide>, which will initiate a major mechanic that will span across the majority of phase 2. This cast is also a very strong raidwide that can hit up to 560k, which is more than twice the max HP of the majority of your party members, so make sure that everyone in the party contributes to mitigating this attack! 

Use your mitigations and head down south of the boss in order to line up in preparation as to what is to come in the next mechanic. The purpose of this line up will be further elaborated later.

## Towers and All Things Ending

Kefka will repeatedly spawn two towers, 90 degrees away from each other, that needs to be soaked by two players each. This happens eight times, which is enough times for your party to get rid of all the debuff stacks. Soaking a tower will rotate the tower 45 degrees clockwise or counterclockwise. You will not know which direction the towers rotate at the start, but once you've soaked the first tower, the towers will always rotate in the same direction throughout this mechanic.

Upon soaking a tower, the spell that you were imprinted will be resolved. Unless your debuff stacks has reached 0, you will always be given a new spell once you resolve one. Which spell you will get is decided by the following factors:
- The towers will always randomly re-assign the players with either a cone or a circle AoE, ensuring that there's always an equal amount of cones and AoE circles.
- Upon resolving the stack markers, the stack markers will be re-assigned on the next tower set if possible.
  - This means that the stack markers will reappear on every odd-numbered sets or even-numbered sets, depending on where the party resolves the stack markers first.
  - If the players has no debuff stacks anymore upon soaking the tower, then the re-assignment of stack markers will be skipped to the next set instead.

Now regarding Kefka himself, during every even-numbered sets, Kefka will use an attack which will target the four closest players with an AoE attack that spawns a clone of himself, which is <strong>Future's End</strong> or <strong>Past's End</strong>.

With the exception of the first set, every odd-numbered sets will have Kefka use <strong>All Things Ending</strong>, which is a halfroom cleave at the front or behind Kefka. Whether it is front or behind Kefka is dependent on whether Kefka used <strong>Future's End</strong> (front) or <strong>Past's End</strong> (behind).

 The direction of Kefka and it clones are baited through proximity, and they will lock into the direction they're facing once you see <strong>All Things Ending</strong> being cast by Kefka. Keep in mind that <strong>Future's End</strong> or <strong>Past's End</strong> is a memory check, so either remember which attack it is by paying attention, or change your log settings so that you can read Kefka's last cast in the chatbox.

So far, this is the information we've gathered:
- Each tower must be soaked by two players.
- Each cone must be baited by one player who is not in a tower.
- Each stack must be soaked by three players.
- Soaking the tower and having a cone marker will not give you a vulnerability, anything else will.
- You cannot resolve stacks on even-numbered sets, because of <strong>Future's End</strong> or <strong>Past's End</strong>.

Now, how are we going to apply this knowledge into solving the mechanic?

## Kroxy-Rinon 341 strategy

In the early weeks, Kroxy came up with an idea to split the role groups between west and east after it was discovered that each role group will always get:
- One stack marker
- All cones or all AoE circles

Rinon further perfected this system by:
- Creating a partner system, which is helpful for melee players during even-numbered sets for knowing whether they have to flex to the other tower or not.
- Making the positions static for odd-numbered sets and partially eliminating the need for a prio system. 
  - By resolving one cone and one AoE circle respectively during odd-numbered sets, you can fix the positions based on what spells they have, compared to when you try to solve two cones or two AoE circles at once.

Now it suddenly makes sense as to why the party needs to line up at the south, because each pair needs to identify whether their group has the stack marker or not. To further explain how this strategy works, we will be using two terms here: tower group and the helper group. The groups with stack markers will start as the tower group, while the groups with two cones or two AoE circles will be the helper group.

And finally, there are differences between the strategies used in Mana and Elemental, which will be further elaborated below on this page. They are minor, but relevant enough that it can cause wipes if the players are unaware of the differences.

### Odd-numbered sets

During odd-numbered sets, the party will resolve two stack markers, one cone and one AoE circle. The cone and AoE circle are fixed, whereas for stacks, the tower priority is support left and DPS right. If both stacks end up in the same role group, then the melee player has to adjust to the other tower.

### Even-numbered sets

On even-numbered sets, you will resolve two cones and two AoE circles. Kefka will perform either <strong>Future's End</strong> or <strong>Past's End</strong> in these sets, which requires four players to bait the AoE attacks. This attack will be baited by the two cone players and the two melee helpers.

As a reminder as to how the priority system works, support is always left side and DPS is always right side. If both players in the role group has the same spell, then the melee adjusts to the other side!

### 341

After the third set, the helper and the tower group must swap roles, so that the initial helper group can do sets 4 to 7. The reason why this is a must is because when a player runs out of debuff stacks, they will no longer get any new spells after soaking the tower. This is normally a good thing, but not when doing so will cause the helper group to end up getting stack markers on the 6th set, which will wipe the party.

The initial tower group must remember the spell they have for the final set, or alternatively, mark themselves with either chain 1/2 (cone) or ignore 1/2 (AoE circle), with the latter being the Mana strategy. You are expected to follow the normal priority system in Elemental for the final set, but in Mana, the chain and ignore markers will override the priority system, so keep that in mind.
